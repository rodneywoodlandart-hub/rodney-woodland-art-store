Artist statement

What is carried forward

My work explores how identity is shaped long before we are old enough to name it.

Through portraiture and symbolism, I examine childhood, family, ancestry, and the quiet ways love, culture, and memory are passed from one generation to the next.

Figures serve as vessels for larger ideas: a child's imagination, a parent's responsibility, an elder's wisdom, and the unseen inheritance carried through family.

Drawing from African visual traditions alongside contemporary figurative painting, I move between observation and metaphor. Some works remain rooted in lived experience, while others transform personal moments into symbolic narratives that invite broader reflection. Whether depicting a grandmother embracing a child or a parent nurturing a growing mind, I am interested in the ways care extends beyond the physical. We do not only feed bodies - we cultivate imagination, identity, and consciousness.

Ultimately, my paintings ask viewers to consider what is carried forward through generations, and how ordinary acts of love become lasting forms of inheritance.

- Rodney Woodland
